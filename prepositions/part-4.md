## Conclusion

Prepositions and postpositions form a universal system for expressing relationships between entities. Across the five languages:

* **English:** relies heavily on prepositions before nouns (*in, on, by, for*).
* **French:** often combines prepositions with articles (*à + le = au, de + le = du*).
* **Italian:** frequently contracts prepositions with articles (*a + il = al, di + il = del*).
* **Arabic:** uses a smaller core set of prepositions but expands meaning through context and grammatical structures (*في، من، إلى، على، لـ، بـ*).
* **Japanese:** expresses equivalent meanings through particles after nouns (*に, で, を, と, から, まで, の, について*).

Together, these systems show that prepositions are not merely spatial words; they encode **space, time, causation, ownership, logic, social relationships, and information structure**.
